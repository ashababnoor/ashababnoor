Typography

Two typefaces: the system sans-serif for the name and role, and a monospace for the terminal-style prompt and the tagline. No fonts are embedded. The banners use the viewer's system fonts, so rendering varies slightly between operating systems.

Principles

A matched monospace pair
The prompt and the tagline are always the same size.
Name to role: 1.75–1.9×
A clear lead for the name without dominating the frame.
Role to tagline: 1.2–1.3×
The tagline is supporting detail, so it's set a step smaller than the role to draw less attention.
Tighter tracking on the name
Large bold type looks loose at default spacing, so the name is tightened slightly: −0.3 at 28px, −1 at 32–34px.
Stroke compensation in light mode
Dark text on a light background looks thinner than light text on a dark one (the irradiation effect). So in light mode, the prompt gets a 0.35px stroke in its own colour, and the $ one in its grey. A heavier font weight wouldn't help, because Menlo, the macOS fallback when Fira Code and SF Mono aren't installed, has only regular and bold.

Vertical spacing

The four lines form three groups by proximity: the prompt, the name and role, and the tagline. The name and role are separated by a small inner gap. The prompt and the tagline each sit one larger group gap away, equal above and below. The difference between the two gaps is what makes the grouping read.

Principles

Hierarchy first, then equal space above and below
The type sizes and gaps were set first, to get the hierarchy right. The frame was then sized to give the text equal space above and below, each a fixed share of the height: 15% on desktop and slim, 12.5% on tablet and mobile. On desktop the text fills the remaining 70% of the height: 97.3 ÷ 0.70 ≈ 139px.
Margin proportions
  • Desktop and slim: top margin 15% of the height, left margin 2 × top. They're shown at actual size on wide screens, so they get more room above, below and to the left.
  • Tablet and mobile: top margin 12.5% of the height, left margin 1.5 × top. The tablet banner is displayed scaled up (about 1–1.6×), so its margins are already generous on screen. On mobile, a wider left margin would push the tagline into the right edge.
Horizontal placement
  • Desktop and tablet: left-aligned, right side open. Keeping the text together on the left reads more easily than spreading it across the frame. The open space also holds the background glow.
  • Slim and mobile: centred. The side margins are equal, so the text as a whole is centred horizontally. On slim, the flush-right tagline ends one left margin from the right edge. The mobile frame is exactly the text block plus the left margin on each side (397px).

Optical alignment

Every line starts on the same visible left edge. Each glyph carries a left side bearing, the built-in space before its outline, which varies by letter, weight and size. The bold “A” of the name has the most. So each line's x is adjusted by a fraction of a pixel until the visible letters line up.

Principles

Three text colours
Primary, secondary and accent, with nothing in between.
Colour sets the hierarchy; size and typeface refine it
The primary colour marks the name, and the secondary colour marks the lines that support it. Within the secondary lines, size and typeface add a further step: the role is larger and set in the sans-serif, so it leads the smaller, monospace tagline.
Contrast is checked over the glow, not only on the plain background
All text meets WCAG 2 AA (4.5:1) in both themes, including where it crosses the glow. On tablet and mobile the tagline runs into the glow, and the quietest grey that keeps 4.5:1 there is almost identical to the secondary colour, so the tagline uses it. The worst case, at the brightest point of the glow on mobile, is 4.9:1 in light and 4.6:1 in dark.
Slim's tagline uses the primary colour
It sits fully on the glow, where the secondary colour drops to 4.2:1. The primary colour keeps 10.5:1 in light and 6.5:1 in dark at the brightest point.

Blur
A Gaussian blur that scales with the frame's height, so every variant has the same softness.
Tagline halo (tablet and mobile)
A faint, blurred copy of the tagline in the background colour sits behind it to separate it from the glow.

Motion and accessibility

Blinking cursor
The cursor blinks every 1.1s, and stops when the viewer has reduced motion turned on (prefers-reduced-motion).
Screen readers
Each SVG has a <title> with the full text, and the README image has alt text.
